Netflix preparation should cover the target team's engineering domain as well as JavaScript, React, and design. Candidates have encountered technical rounds and detailed discussions about their work; concentrating only on culture questions would miss a substantial part of those experiences.

## Coding and system design

- Rehearse JavaScript and React logic, explaining state changes and the behavior of your implementation.
- Confirm whether coding involves visible UI: one full-stack applicant was told to expect JavaScript and React without UI implementation.
- Prepare a system design discussion and investigate the product or engineering domain named by the team.

A data-driven UI can be practiced with [Data Table](https://www.greatfrontend.com/questions/user-interface/data-table?utm_source=frontendinterviewhandbook&utm_medium=referral&gnrs=frontendinterviewhandbook), whose base requirements cover pagination. This is a related exercise, not a claim that Netflix asks that exact question; sorting, filtering, and remote loading would be follow-ups.

## Project and manager discussions

Past conversations have explored a project the candidate was proud of, disagreements with another engineer, and feedback from a manager. Choose examples where you can explain your decision and what changed afterward. Netflix's [Culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture) provides context for discussing collaboration and feedback, rather than a technical-round schedule.

## Insider tips from the GreatFrontEnd community

These tips were shared by [GreatFrontEnd](https://www.greatfrontend.com/?utm_source=frontendinterviewhandbook&utm_medium=referral&gnrs=frontendinterviewhandbook) users who have completed interviews with Netflix.

**January 2026**:

> Netflix changes their process every couple of months. One thing that's been consistent in recent loops: you need to code really confidently and quickly — if they sense you're hesitant or making mistakes even while trying to debug, they'll just tell you to stop the interview. Make sure your live-coding fluency is at the level where you can think out loud without getting stuck.

**January 2026**:

> The hiring manager round at Netflix is mainly behavioral around your experience: "project you're proud of" (with a deepdive but not super deep), handling issues with another engineer, what's the negative feedback you got the most from your manager, etc. Knowing their values — the [Culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture) and the Dream Team concept — is helpful, though it didn't translate into a pass for me.
>
> Recruiter screening was pretty quick and basic — matching technologies and experience, no technical questions besides "what stack you've worked with" and a high-level summary of past projects.
